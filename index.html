An ESG label changes what a fund owns, not how it votes

Shelby L. Riggs · Stewardship analysis · SEC Form N-PX, 2023–2026

Every U.S. mutual fund and ETF must tell the SEC how it voted at every company meeting. This report reads three years of those filings for 22 large-cap stock funds — the flagship and ESG-labeled funds of Vanguard, BlackRock, and State Street, two large active managers (Fidelity and Capital Group), and six ESG-dedicated fund families — to ask where stewardship actually happens. The short answer: an ESG fund inside a big family votes exactly like that family's flagship, the real divide is between managers, and support for environmental and social proposals shrank almost everywhere between 2024 and 2026.

Labels

Inside a fund family, the ESG fund votes the same way as the flagship

Each of the Big Three, and Fidelity, sells ESG-labeled versions of its U.S. index funds. On the same shareholder proposal at the same meeting, those ESG funds cast a different vote from their family's flagship almost never: 0.1% of the time for iShares ESG Aware (860 proposals compared), 0.2% for SPDR S&P 500 ESG, 0.4% for Vanguard ESG U.S. Stock, and 0% for Fidelity's U.S. Sustainability Index. The two exceptions are small: iShares Paris-Aligned Climate differs on 3.0% of shareholder proposals, and Fidelity's actively managed Sustainable U.S. Equity Fund on 5.6% compared with Contrafund.

For scale, the Vanguard 500 Index Fund and the Calvert U.S. Large-Cap Core Responsible Index Fund — an ESG index fund from a different manager — disagree on 64.3% of the shareholder proposals they both voted. The label changes which companies a fund holds; the family's stewardship team decides how it votes.

Managers

The real divide is between managers: from 0% to 80% support for pro-ESG proposals

Counting only shareholder proposals that ask companies for more climate, social, or human-rights action or disclosure (“pro-ESG”), Vanguard's three funds supported none of them: 0 of 579 for the Vanguard 500 Index Fund. BlackRock's S&P 500, ESG Aware, and ESG Screened ETFs supported about 2% (its Paris-Aligned Climate ETF 9.3%), State Street's SPDR funds 9–11%, and Fidelity's index funds under 3%. At the other end, Calvert's index fund supported 80.5%, Domini 79.9%, and Green Century 79.5%.

Actively managed mainstream funds sit in between but closer to the index giants: Fidelity Contrafund at 14.5%, The Growth Fund of America at 4.3%. Fidelity's Sustainable U.S. Equity Fund, at 27.5%, is the ESG fund inside a mainstream family that votes most differently from its siblings.

Big Three

Big Three support for pro-ESG proposals, already low, kept falling

Among the four S&P 500 index flagships, only State Street's gave pro-ESG proposals meaningful support, and it fell from 12.6% in the 2023–24 proxy year to 8.2% and then 8.7%. iShares went from 2.8% to 1.1% to 0.9%, Fidelity's 500 Index Fund from 4.7% to zero, and Vanguard stayed at zero all three years.

Share of votes cast FOR on pro-ESG shareholder proposals, by reporting year (July 1–June 30).

The ballot

Fewer environmental and social proposals reached a vote, and more of them were anti-ESG

The four S&P 500 index flagships hold nearly the same companies, so the proposals they vote on track the ballot itself. Each voted on about 539 shareholder proposals in the 2023–24 proxy year, 388 in 2024–25, and 332 in 2025–26. Pro-ESG proposals fell faster, from about 279 to 113 — a 60% drop.

Proposals pushing the other way — asking companies to report the risks of their DEI programs or climate commitments, or to review “politicized” banking — fell much less (from about 36 to 27), so their share of environmental and social proposals rose from 10.6% to 17.9%.

Average number of shareholder proposals voted per S&P 500 index flagship (Vanguard 500, iShares Core S&P 500, SPDR Portfolio S&P 500, Fidelity 500 Index), by proposal direction.

The ballot

Almost no fund votes for anti-ESG proposals — including the ones that vote against ESG proposals

Anti-ESG proposals received 0.0% support from every Big Three and active-manager fund in the panel (706 and 345 votes). The ESG families gave them 3.2%. So the Big Three's low support for pro-ESG proposals is not a mirror image: they vote against shareholder proposals on environmental and social topics from both directions.

ESG families

Even ESG specialists supported fewer pro-ESG proposals in 2025–26

Calvert's index fund supported 86.1% of pro-ESG proposals in 2023–24 and 67.0% in 2025–26. Domini went from 81.9% to 67.8% and Green Century from 81.5% to 71.8%. Parnassus moved the other way, to 78.3%. Because anti-ESG proposals are counted separately, the decline is not an artifact of a changing ballot mix.

The Impax U.S. Sustainable Economy Fund is the outlier: after supporting 74–76% of pro-ESG proposals in the first two years, it voted against 142 of its 143 shareholder proposals in 2025–26. The filing records the votes but not the reason.

Pay & boards

Pushback on pay and boards comes from ESG families and active managers, not the index giants

On say-on-pay votes, Domini voted against 87.8% of the time, Calvert's index fund 65.0%, and Green Century 64.8%. Among mainstream funds, The Growth Fund of America opposed 18.4% of pay packages; every Big Three fund opposed 4.3% or fewer. On director elections, Domini voted against or withheld on 58.1% of nominees and Green Century 52.4%, while no Big Three fund exceeded 4.9%.

Share of management say-on-pay and director-election votes cast that were not FOR (against, withhold, or abstain).

Governance

The Big Three do back shareholder proposals — on governance, not environmental or social issues

Big Three funds supported 37.0% of shareholder proposals on shareholder rights and defenses (proxy access, special meetings, declassified boards), nearly ten times their 3.8% support for pro-ESG proposals. Active managers show the same pattern more strongly: 69.4% on shareholder rights, 6.6% on pro-ESG. Only the ESG families support both kinds at similar rates.

Share of votes cast FOR on shareholder proposals, by fund group: pro-ESG proposals, all governance proposals, and the SEC's “shareholder rights and defenses” category.

Active managers

Capital Group's funds sharply increased their support for governance proposals in 2025–26

The Growth Fund of America supported 43.2% of governance shareholder proposals in 2023–24, 44.7% in 2024–25, and 62.1% in 2025–26. Washington Mutual Investors Fund rose from 31.4% to 50.6%. Their support for pro-ESG proposals stayed between 1% and 6% throughout, so the shift is about governance, not ESG.

About the data

Source. SEC Form N-PX filings, pulled from EDGAR by scripts/fetch_npx.js. Since July 2023 funds file N-PX in a structured format that records, for every proposal, the company, meeting date, proposal text, the SEC's topic categories, who proposed it, how the fund voted, and management's recommendation. Each filing covers one July 1–June 30 reporting year; this panel covers the three years ending June 2024, 2025, and 2026.

What one row is. One fund's vote on one proposal at one company meeting: 303,589 rows for 22 funds, 2,083 companies, and 12 calendar quarters of meetings (2023-Q3 to 2026-Q2). The funds are all U.S. large-cap stock funds, chosen so they vote at largely the same companies.

Cleaning. Some filers report one proposal in several blocks (one per voting team); those are merged into one row, and a block that exactly repeats another is counted once. Shares some investors vote under their own chosen policy (pass-through voting) appear as small separate blocks; any block under 10% of the fund's largest block at a meeting is left out, so the data reflects the fund manager's own decision. Vote spellings are normalized. Details and counts are in the repository README.

Proposal direction. The SEC categories say what a proposal is about but not which way it points. scripts/stance_rules.js labels each shareholder proposal pro-ESG, anti-ESG, unclear (topics both camps file, such as charitable giving), or governance, using ordered keyword rules; every label records the rule that produced it, and data/proposal_stance_review.csv lists all 2,184 distinct proposal texts for review.

How the rates are computed. Support is the share of votes cast FOR, out of FOR, AGAINST, ABSTAIN, and WITHHOLD. Opposition on management items is the share of those votes that were not FOR. Rates pool fund-level votes, so a proposal voted by three funds counts three times. Head-to-head comparisons match proposals by company, meeting date, and proposal text. Every number on this page is computed by scripts/analyze.js into data/findings.json.

Data: SEC EDGAR, Form N-PX. Built by Shelby L. Riggs with Claude Code.
